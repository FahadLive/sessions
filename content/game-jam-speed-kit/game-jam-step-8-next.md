# Step 8 (10 min): Where to go next

You have a game, a link, and 3 hours of muscle memory. The only thing left is deciding what happens next weekend.

## Find a jam

- **[itch.io/jams](https://itch.io/jams)** — hundreds of jams, filterable by "starting soon" and length. The 48-hour weekend jams are the natural next step after today.
- **GMTK Game Jam** — the big annual one (summer, 48 hours, theme announced live). Search for it on itch.io when it's running; the whole internet plays along.
- **Ludum Dare** — the old guard, twice a year, with a compo (solo, 48h) and a jam (team, longer). Run for decades; the archive of decades of games is a design education by itself.

> **Key insight:** a jam deadline is a gift. Today worked *because* of the timer, not despite it. The next game you finish will also be finished by a countdown.

## Learn more Godot

| Resource | What it's for |
| -------- | ------------- |
| **`F1` inside Godot** | The built-in manual for every node and function. Seriously — this is the underrated one |
| **Official Godot docs** (docs.godotengine.org) | Step-by-step guides, and the class reference your F1 searches into |
| **GDQuest** | Written and video courses, strong fundamentals, free tier is deep |
| **Brackeys** | The famous beginner YouTube series — fast, friendly, Godot 4 |
| **The Godot Discord** | Live help; read the channel norms, ask with your error message attached |
| **r/godot** | News, workflows, and "how would you do X" threads |

Pick **one**. Rotating between five tutorials is how people stay beginners for a year.

## Build the habit that saves projects

1. **Make a starter template.** Copy today's empty-but-configured project folder (Compatibility renderer, 960×540, your four folders) somewhere safe and delete its `.godot` folder — that's your template. Next jam starts at minute 2 of setup, not minute 12.
2. **Zip a backup every hour.** Literally every jam hour: zip the project folder, name it with the time (`jam-1400.zip`). Costs 15 seconds; has saved more jam games than any other habit in this handbook.
3. **The cut list travels.** Every jam: write the "NOT TODAY" list first. It's not a constraint on creativity — it's the thing that lets creativity *ship*.

## Using AI well (you'll want to — that's fine)

AI assistants are genuinely useful for beginners now, and genuinely capable of writing code you can't defend. The rules that keep them on the right side:

- **Say who you are, up front:** *"I'm a beginner using Godot 4.x, GDScript. Explain simply."* It changes every answer you get.
- **Paste the exact error and the relevant script.** Not "my coin doesn't work" — the red line and the 10 lines around it. Vague questions get vague answers; specific ones often get the actual fix.
- **One small change at a time, with an explanation.** "Explain what this does before I paste it" is a complete sentence. Ten explained one-liners teach; one mysterious 80-line script teaches nothing.
- **Don't paste code you can't explain.** If you can't say what each line does, you don't have code — you have a debt with interest.
- **If the fix fails twice, stop.** Check the built-in docs (`F1`) or ask the facilitator. Two failed AI fixes usually mean the AI is confidently wrong about your setup — a human who can see your screen will spot it in 30 seconds.

> **Key insight:** AI is a **fast pair programmer who never gets tired and sometimes hallucinates**. Treat it like one: interesting suggestions, verify before shipping, and keep your hands on the keyboard. Your hands on the keyboard is the entire point of today.

## Checkpoint

Pick one and say it to the person next to you:

- [ ] A jam, with its dates, in your calendar
- [ ] One learning resource, bookmarked
- [ ] A backup habit (hourly zips — start today, back up this project now)

Now go send your link to someone. Then close the laptop — you've shipped a game today, and that's not a small thing.
